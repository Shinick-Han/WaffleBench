Which dies are worth a paid review?

A scanned wafer lot has thousands of care sites, but each SEM review costs equipment budget, so only a few dozen can be reviewed. The question is which sites to pay for so that the most real defects of interest (DOI) are confirmed. Dies that are never reviewed stay unknown.

Evidence source: the original frozen synthetic Inspection v3 study (generator-made wafers and sensor responses), read from web/data/inspection-v3.json. Not real fab data, SEM-image accuracy or yield.

Research chain

What was hypothesised, what was recorded, and what could be measured next. Only the first two are evidence.

  1. Hypothesis · preregistered

    A better review policy confirms more DOI at the same budget

    Before the test campaign, one candidate policy was chosen and a minimum relative gain over a comparator was fixed as the target.

  2. Result and diagnostic · recorded

    Target outcome, then why the count stays small

    A separate posthoc diagnostic of the same frozen ledgers (it tunes nothing) found that the number of affordable reviews, not ranking, is the binding limit in the high-recall scenarios, while in the process-shift and low-contrast scenarios many DOI are never offered by the optical scan (most of them in low contrast) and the classifier is overconfident. Figures: INSPECTION_QUALITY_DIAGNOSTICS.md and the misses and review budget page.

  3. Proposed next study · not executed

    Can shift-aware screening and admission reach DOI the scan never offered?

    Re-ranking alone cannot recover sites that were never admitted as candidates. A new prospective study could test shift-aware screening, optical retest and candidate admission in shifted scenarios, with every retest and admission charged within the same total budget as the reviews, against the frozen v3 policy. It would need its own preregistration, fresh paired holdout lots (the v3 test lots are consumed), recorded costs and independent verification.

    No data, run or result exists for this proposal. Its outcome is unknown.
